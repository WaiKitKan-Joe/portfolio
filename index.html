Equity Research and Corporate Valuation

EOG Resources Equity Research and Monte Carlo DCF Valuation

Independent equity research combining fundamental analysis, probabilistic valuation, risk assessment and technical monitoring

An independent equity research report on EOG Resources that evaluates the company's operating performance, investment thesis, catalysts, risks and valuation. The project applies a 50,000-path Monte Carlo FCFF model, an adjusted-CAPM cost-of-capital framework, sensitivity analysis and an operating KPI dashboard to assess the range and key drivers of EOG's equity value.

Research Methodology

  • Evaluated EOG's business model, production profile, realised commodity prices, operating costs and capital-allocation strategy.
  • Developed a five-year FCFF valuation model covering sales growth, EBIT margin, tax, depreciation, capital expenditure and working capital.
  • Estimated the cost of equity using raw and Blume-adjusted beta assumptions and calculated a central weighted average cost of capital.
  • Ran 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations using triangular distributions for major operating and valuation assumptions.
  • Assessed catalysts, thesis-breaking risks, valuation sensitivity and quarterly monitoring KPIs.

Key Findings

  • Central model-derived fair value: US$225.54 per share.
  • Reference closing price: US$157.88 on 21 October 2026.
  • Model-derived capital upside: approximately 42.9%, excluding dividends.
  • EBIT margin was the strongest positive valuation driver, while WACC and capital-expenditure intensity were the principal negative drivers.
  • The research stance was constructive but high risk because the valuation remained sensitive to commodity prices, margins, reinvestment and discount-rate assumptions.

Project Outputs

  • Percentile-based scenarios.

Fixed Income and Econometrics

U.S. Treasury Yield Forecasting

Yield-curve factors, AutoReg modelling and expanding-window backtesting

A forecasting project for the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield using DGS10, DGS5, DGS2 and DGS1MO factors. The project compares a four-factor OLS model, AR(10) and an unchanged-yield benchmark over a 10-observation forecast horizon.

Methodology

  • Retrieved Treasury-yield data directly from FRED.
  • Estimated a four-factor OLS forecasting model.
  • Applied Ljung-Box, White and HAC diagnostic procedures.
  • Backtested OLS and AR(10) using expanding estimation windows.

Key Findings

  • Latest DGS10 forecast: 5.0322%.
  • Estimated 71.12% probability that DGS10 exceeds 4.955%.
  • Factor-model OOS R²: -31.1357%.
  • AR(10) OOS R²: -67.0541%; the persistence benchmark performed best.

Corporate Valuation and Risk Analysis

OSK Monte Carlo DCF Enterprise Value Analysis

Probabilistic enterprise valuation using 50,000 DCF simulations

A five-year discounted cash flow model for Oshkosh Corporation that uses Monte Carlo simulation to estimate a distribution of possible enterprise values. The model varies sales growth, EBIT margin, WACC, perpetual growth, capital expenditure, tax and working-capital assumptions to assess valuation uncertainty and compare model-implied value with the current enterprise value.

Methodology

  • Forecast five years of sales, EBIT, NOPAT and free cash flow to the firm.
  • Model uncertain DCF inputs with triangular probability distributions.
  • Run 50,000 simulations and discount cash flows using simulated WACC.
  • Estimate terminal value using the Gordon growth model.
  • Measure valuation sensitivity and the probability of exceeding current enterprise value.

Key Results

  • Mean model-implied EV: $9,027.50 million.
  • Median model-implied EV: $8,986.06 million.
  • 5th to 95th percentile range: $5,750.28 million to $12,453.60 million.
  • Probability that model-implied EV exceeds $9,060 million: 48.652%.
  • WACC showed the strongest measured negative relationship with enterprise value.

Machine Learning and Investment Analysis

Global Technology Companies K-Means Clustering

Financial peer segmentation using fundamentals, valuation, risk and momentum

An unsupervised-learning project that groups global technology companies into financially similar peer segments. The analysis winsorises extreme observations, standardises seven financial features, evaluates alternative cluster counts using silhouette scores, and interprets the final five-cluster solution for investment screening and peer analysis.

Methodology

  • Downloaded the 2026 global technology fundamentals dataset through the Kaggle API.
  • Selected company size, R&D intensity, operating margin, trailing and forward P/E, beta and 52-week momentum.
  • Clipped observations at the 1st and 99th percentiles and standardised all features.
  • Tested K-Means solutions from k = 2 to 15 using silhouette scores.
  • Generated company assignments and cluster-level standardised profiles.

Key Results

  • Selected five clusters with the highest silhouette score of 0.353.
  • Identified mega-cap profitable leaders with market-cap and margin scores of +3.17 and +1.17.
  • Separated high-momentum, higher-risk companies with momentum of +2.03 and beta of +1.08.
  • Identified R&D-intensive, low-profitability companies with R&D of +0.88 and margin of -1.67.
  • Isolated expensive, high-beta innovation companies with trailing P/E of +3.18 and beta of +1.14.
